1. Would You Borrow It?

Opening decision
The purchase: You need a $1,200 laptop for school and work. You have enough savings to buy it today, but doing that would use a large part of your savings.

Option A — Pay Today

$1,200 today using debit/cash.

No debt. Your savings drops immediately.

Option B — 12-Month Financing

$108/month for 12 months

Scheduled total: $1,296.

Option C — 24-Month Financing

$58/month for 24 months

Scheduled total: $1,392.

Option D — Credit Card

Put $1,200 on a card at 24% APR.

Final cost depends on how quickly you repay the balance.

Which option would you choose right now, and why? What information are you paying attention to?

3. Sort It: What Kind of Payment Is This?

Self-checking sort

Installment Credit

Borrow a set amount and repay it through scheduled payments over a set period.

Revolving Credit

Borrow up to a credit limit, repay, and use available credit again.

Not Credit

Use money you already have rather than borrowing.

Use the definitions above to sort each example as installment credit, revolving credit, or not credit. You get three checks. The first two checks tell you only how many are correct. The third reveals the answers and locks the sort.
